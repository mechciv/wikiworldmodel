# The climate: a roadmap to the strongest method of each era

Status: proposed, 27 November 2026. It follows the row *The climate of each month* of
[MODELS.md](MODELS.md#the-quantities-in-the-order-of-their-causes) and the
[twenty-fourth slice](../slices/TWENTY-FOURTH-SLICE.md), which gave the climate of the deep past a spread and found
how little the evidence the world held could say of it. Nothing here is built. The works named as candidates were
not read for this plan: only their hosts' pages were, for [their licences](#candidate-datasets-and-their-licences).
Each is read, and its licence checked again, by the slice that brings it in.

## Why

Every quantity below the climate reads it: the water and the rivers, what grew, where each taxon could live, what a
farmer could sow, what a house was built against. And the climate is the weakest of the world's large fields:

- **One run of another's model for each time,** every 10 Myr before the last glacial cycle, with no spread
  (`field:earth/climate`, `field:earth/climate-late-quaternary`).
- **No evidence conditions what is drawn.** The twenty-fourth slice set a conditioned climate beside the run
  (`model:earth/climate-conditioned`), and nothing drawn reads it.
- **Two climates that disagree.** The run was tuned to one curve of the Earth's mean warmth and the world holds
  another: they lie 2.8 °C apart as a root mean square, and at the Hell Creek the conditioned climate is colder than
  the run where fossil leaves side with the run.
- **Whole stretches of time have the wrong kind of answer.** The ice ages before 120 ka are drawn with the climate of
  the present, and the last two thousand years are two keyframes.

The aim: **at any place and time, the twelve months of warmth and rain with a spread, by the strongest method that
time allows, scored on evidence held out, and one answer where there are now two.**

## The method

The strongest method is the same in kind at every time, and it is the one [MODELS.md](MODELS.md) already asks for:

1. **A prior from physics:** a climate model's answer to the Sun, the CO₂, the land and its heights, and the ice.
2. **Conditioned on evidence measured at places,** each kind through a model of how it records the climate, and
   compared in the evidence's own terms where that can be done: an isotope ratio, a leaf's shape, a ring's width.
3. **Scored on evidence held out,** by region and by era, beside a baseline with no causes.

What changes with age is how dense and how well dated the evidence is, how well the model's inputs are known, and
whether a correction learned at the present still holds. That gives the eras their different strongest methods:

| Time | The strongest method | Evidence at places | What limits it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1940 to now | A reanalysis: a weather model with the measurements taken in every few hours; station grids brought to 1 km | stations, balloons, satellites | rain in mountains and where stations are few |
| About 1400 to 1940 | A reanalysis from few inputs: surface pressure alone, or early instruments with documents and tree rings | early instruments, ships' logs, chronicles, tree rings | cover outside Europe and the North Atlantic |
| The last 2,000 years | Proxies taken into a model's ensemble year by year; drought atlases from tree rings | tree rings, corals, ice cores, cave deposits, layered lake beds, dated to the year | proxies of one season, mostly of the northern land; rain far weaker than warmth |
| 21 ka to 2 ka | The same, over the deglaciation, at steps of centuries; runs through time; brought to 1 km | pollen, lake levels, cave deposits, cores of the sea bed, ice cores, the limits of glaciers | dates to centuries; the ice sheets' height and where their meltwater ran |
| 2.6 Ma to 21 ka | Model snapshots joined by the orbit, the CO₂ and the ice, and corrected against the present | ice cores to 800 ka, the isotopes of the deep sea, long pollen and loess sequences | no CO₂ from ice before 800 ka; few long records on land |
| 23 to 2.6 Ma | Several models on one reconstruction of the land, held to proxies site by site | plankton's chemistry, boron's isotopes for CO₂, the shapes of leaves, fossil floras | CO₂ to a factor of about 1.5; the heights of ranges; the seaways |
| 145 to 23 Ma | Several models for chosen windows; proxies taken in where they are dense | membrane lipids, clumped isotopes, the isotopes of well-kept shells, leaf margins, soil carbonates, palms and crocodilians as bounds on frost | the models' polar winters, too cold; proxies of coasts and seas; old heights |
| 540 to 145 Ma | One run a stage on a map of the land; isotopes taken in for the Earth's mean and its gradient | the isotopes of conodonts and brachiopods, clumped isotopes, coals, salts, tills | the map of the land itself, its longitudes and relief; CO₂ to a factor of two or more |
| Before 540 Ma | An energy balance for the state of the whole Earth | glacial beds with their old latitudes | no trusted map of the continents: no climate of a region at all |

Three things hold across the eras, and order the steps below:

- **A correction against the present is the largest gain for a region,** and it holds only while the land is nearly
  today's. That is why the last glacial cycle is far better known than the Miocene.
- **A bound is strong evidence.** A palm, a crocodilian or a forest says more of the coldest month, and more firmly,
  than most numbers do.
- **Rain is a step behind warmth everywhere.** It follows the relief and the winds, which are what is least known.

## Where it stands

| Time | What answers today | The gap to the strongest method |
|---|---|---|
| 1850 to now | The climate observed in 1901–1930, as the last keyframe of `field:earth/climate-late-quaternary`; nothing year by year | no measured years, no months finer than 1° |
| The last 2,000 years | The same field's last keyframes, 1,000 years apart | no year is warmer, colder, wetter or drier than another: no drought, no cold decade |
| 120 ka to 2 ka | Beyer et al. 2020: a model's months every 1,000 to 2,000 years, corrected to the present; no spread; nothing south of 60°S | no spread; no evidence conditions it; no second model |
| 800 ka to 120 ka | The deep past's run of the present, which has today's ice | the cycles are missing, though the world holds their CO₂, their sea level and Antarctica's warmth |
| 10 Ma to 800 ka | The same run, blended towards the run of 10 Ma | no cycles; no orbit in the world but today's |
| 540 to 10 Ma | Li et al. 2022, one run every 10 Myr; beside it the conditioned climate with a spread, which nothing drawn reads | no warmth measured at a place; one model; the level of the curve against the level of the runs unsettled |
| Before 540 Ma | The planet's phases; the energy balance for the snowballs | none to close: see [what it will not do](#what-it-will-not-do) |

## What the twenty-fourth slice taught

- **Evidence before machinery.** A chain of fitted stages was built to read coals, salts, reefs and tills, and
  they narrow a band by an eighth or more on 4.7% of the land. The next gain is in what is read, not in how.
- **The present's error is not the deep past's.** A spread read where the climate is measured holds there (nine in
  ten) and is too narrow where the land is a drawn map.
- **A level must be settled before a centre is drawn.** Moving the run to the world's curve made the Hell Creek
  colder than its leaves allow.
- **A mechanism that tells less than a plain mean has not earned its place.** The energy balance, as it is, is one.

## The steps

Each is a slice or less, with pass criteria and hold-outs of its own, as
[CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#larger-work-slices) asks. They are in the order proposed: evidence first,
then the times with the wrong kind of answer, then the method.

| # | Step | Time it serves | New dataset | Changes what is drawn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bounds on frost from fossils already held | 250 Ma to now | none | no |
| 2 | Warmth measured at places | 540 Ma to 10 Ma | one or two, and claims | no |
| 3 | The level settled | 540 Ma to 10 Ma | none, or one | no |
| 4 | The glacial cycles to 800 ka | 800 ka to 120 ka | one | yes |
| 5 | A spread for the last glacial cycle | 120 ka to now | two or three | no |
| 6 | The last two thousand years, year by year | 1 CE to 1850 | one | yes, by the year |
| 7 | The measured years | 1850 to now | one or two | yes, by the year |
| 8 | Rain that knows the relief | all | one, as a check | scenes |
| 9 | The cycles before 800 ka | 5 Ma to 800 ka | one, or an orbit | yes |
| 10 | A second model for the deep past | 540 Ma to 2.6 Ma | one or more | no |
| 11 | Evidence taken into an ensemble | all with an ensemble | none | no |
| 12 | What is drawn switched | all | none | yes |

### 1. Bounds on frost from fossils already held

Crocodilians and palms do not live through hard winters, and the Paleobiology Database's finds, which the build
reads, say where each stood. Each find is an observation of the coldest month at its place, as a bound, in the way
the fossil forests are read now.

- **The thresholds are parameters with sources,** to be read before they are written: the works usually named are
  Markwick (1998) for crocodilians and Greenwood and Wing (1995) for palms.
- **A find is set against the effort that could have found it** (decision 14, the twenty-second slice): where land
  vertebrates were collected and no crocodilian was, that says a little; where nothing was collected, nothing.
- **Pass:** bounds held out by block and by era are met more often by the model that reads the others than by the
  run; and at the Hell Creek, on Ellesmere Island and on Alaska's north slope the chance of a winter above freezing
  rises to what their finds require.

### 2. Warmth measured at places

A property for the mean of the year and for the coldest and warmest month at a place, measured: by the shapes of
leaves, by isotopes, by membrane lipids. Two ways in, both needed:

- **Claims, for the well-known sites:** the Hell Creek's leaves first, read again from their source, with the method
  and its error. These are the places the model is judged at.
- **A dataset, for the many:** a compilation of proxy measurements with their sites and ages, read as rows. The
  proxies of the sea's surface condition the sea beside the land, and through the run's pattern the land.
- **In the proxy's own terms where its model is simple:** a leaf-margin share and not a temperature, when the rows
  give it.
- **Pass:** proxies held out by 40° block and by era are told better than by the run with its present spread; the
  Hell Creek's band holds the leaves' range.

### 3. The level settled

The world holds one curve of the Earth's mean warmth, the runs were tuned to an earlier form of it, and a third
reconstruction, PhanDA's, is warmer by up to 13 °C. With steps 1 and 2 there is evidence to choose by.

- **The levels are competing readings,** scored on the same held-out bounds and proxies: the curve's, the runs' own,
  the two with the energy balance, and PhanDA's if its licence is as
  [the table](#candidate-datasets-and-their-licences) has it.
- **The energy balance stays only if it earns it** (decision 12): unlifted, or with a geography of its own.
- **Pass:** one level is preferred with its score beside the others', and the conditioned climate's centre is no
  further from the well-known places than the run's. Until then nothing drawn reads the centre.

### 4. The glacial cycles to 800 ka

Krapp et al. (2021) is the same kind of reconstruction as the field of the last 120 kyr, for 800 kyr, and is marked
open in [DATA-OUTLINE.md](../DATA-OUTLINE.md). It is brought in as that field was, and answers where the run of the
present answers now.

- **It meets what the world already holds for those years:** the CO₂ of the ice cores, the sea level's stack and
  Antarctica's warmth, each of which becomes a check.
- **Golden images of those years change,** and the pull request says which and why.
- **Pass:** every layer that reads the climate answers through the span; at the keyframes shared with the field of
  the last 120 kyr the two agree within a stated tolerance.

### 5. A spread for the last glacial cycle

That field is corrected to the present, so its error there is nought by making, and the present cannot give its
spread. Its spread comes from evidence of its own time and from other models:

- **Pollen's reconstructions and the reanalyses of the deglaciation observe it** (decision 13): another's
  reconstruction is an observation with its own error, never the prior.
- **Other models' runs of the glacial maximum and the mid-Holocene** give how far models differ where the land is
  nearly today's.
- **Pass:** sites held out by block lie in the nine-in-ten band about nine times in ten, at the glacial maximum and
  in the mid-Holocene.

### 6. The last two thousand years, year by year

A field of each year's departure from the climate of its time, with members, from a reanalysis of proxies. It is
what the human track needs: a failed harvest, a decade of drought, a cold summer.

- **Kept as departures,** on a coarse grid, over the months the climate field already gives.
- **Events the records hold are checks, not inputs:** a drought or a cold year dated by a chronicle is held against
  the members of its year.
- **Pass:** the members' spread holds proxies left out; years the records name as dry or cold are so in more members
  than not.

### 7. The measured years

The same field carried on through the years of instruments, from a reanalysis and station grids, and a finer
climate of the present for scenes.

- **The measured years are the test bed for every step above.** Any method here can be run blind where the answer
  is known: read only what a proxy would have recorded, and score against what was measured.
- **Pass:** the year-by-year field and the proxy-based one agree over the years they share, within the members'
  spread.

### 8. Rain that knows the relief

A scene's climate is brought to its heights by one lapse rate, with the node's rain at every height: no rain
shadow, no wet windward slope. A model of the world's own, from the winds the fields already keep and the relief,
spreads a node's rain over its ground.

- **Checked against a 1 km climate of the present,** which it does not read.
- **Pass:** on today's ranges held out, the rain of the windward and lee slopes is told better than by the node's
  rain alone.

### 9. The cycles before 800 ka

Before the ice cores the cycles follow from the orbit, the CO₂ and the ice. Two ways, the first much the cheaper:

- **Another's emulator, brought in:** PALEO-PGEM-Series gives months of warmth and rain every 1,000 years for 5 Myr
  at 1°, and its licence is open. It would answer as the fields of steps 4 and 5 do, and be held to the same checks.
- **An emulator of the world's own:** a climate as a function of the orbit, the CO₂ and the ice, fitted to the
  snapshots of step 4 and held to the isotopes of the deep sea. Then the first observes it.
- **The world needs an orbit for the second:** a solution for the eccentricity, the tilt and the precession through
  time, as a computed field with its source.
- **The runs observe it and are not its prior,** which keeps decision 13.
- **Pass:** with the last 120 kyr held out, the emulator tells its months better than the mean climate of the cycles
  does; and what is brought in agrees with step 4's field over the 800 kyr they share, within a stated tolerance.

### 10. A second model for the deep past

The pattern's error in the deep past cannot be read at the present. How far two models differ on the same land is
the nearest measure of it.

- **A second set of runs through the Phanerozoic:** the HadCM3L runs kept as PhanDA's priors are open, with the
  air's temperature and the rain, in several sets of experiments. That is also what an ensemble for step 11 needs.
- **The ensembles made for the Pliocene and the early Eocene,** model by model, where each one's licence allows.
- **Pass:** the spread between models, used as the pattern's error, holds the bounds and proxies of steps 1 and 2
  about as often as it says.

### 11. Evidence taken into an ensemble

With an ensemble (step 10) and evidence at places (steps 1 and 2), the chain of the twenty-fourth slice gives way to
the method proper: the members weighed and moved by every observation at once, each through its own model. The beds
become one kind of evidence among several, and the stages that did not earn their place go.

- **Pass:** every score of the twenty-fourth slice is met or bettered with fewer fitted numbers.

### 12. What is drawn switched

The biome, the water, the rivers and the scenes read the central member, and the members after it.

- **Only when:** the level is settled (3); the centre is no further from the well-known places than the run at any
  of them; and the bands hold on evidence held out in the deep past, not only at the present.
- **Together with the sea's chance and the height of the land** (the twenty-first to twenty-third slices), so that
  the ground and the climate change once.

## What it will not do

- **Run a climate model in the build.** The world reads others' runs, and fits emulators to them: a run of the
  whole Earth is days of a large machine, and the build is minutes.
- **Bring in what has no open licence,** or work around it: a dataset whose host states none is asked after, not
  assumed.
- **Give a region a climate before 540 Ma.** With no trusted map of the continents the layer answers with the state
  of the whole Earth, and says so.
- **Say what the weather was on a day** before it was measured. The months, and from step 6 the year's departure,
  are the claim; the days are a generator's, and marked so.
- **Turn a proxy database into thousands of records.** A compilation is a dataset read as rows; the well-known
  sites are claims.

## Open questions

1. **The pattern and decision 13.** Until step 9 or 10, the run is the shape of the prior at every place. Is an
   emulator fitted to others' runs the world's own model, or another's at one remove?
2. **One member, many fields.** A member's CO₂, sea level, ice and climate should be one draw. The twenty-second to
   twenty-fourth slices each made members of their own.
3. **How a year is kept.** Two thousand years of departures on the climate's grid is a large file to fetch for a
   click. A coarser grid, or a few patterns with a weight for each year?
4. **Which season a proxy speaks for.** Most record a summer or a growing season, and reading them as the year's
   mean moves the centre.
5. **What is drawn first.** Step 4 changes pictures soon and safely. Step 12 changes every picture of the deep past.
   Should anything between them be drawn, such as a spread shown as a texture?
6. **Licences that are neither open nor closed.** The Open Government Licence is not on the list, and NOAA's pages
   state none for several reanalyses. Are those asked after, or left?
